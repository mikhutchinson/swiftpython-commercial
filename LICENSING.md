# SDK licensing

SwiftPython commercial and SiriusMarkdown use the same Commercial SDK License
1.0. Eligibility depends on business revenue, not team size.

| Trailing twelve-month business revenue | Tier |
|---|---|
| Below USD 100,000 | Community — free commercial use |
| USD 100,000 to below USD 1,000,000 | Indie — paid license |
| USD 1,000,000 or more | Business — paid license |
| SDK, runtime or developer-service redistribution | Separate OEM agreement at any revenue |

[Read the full license](LICENSE). Its terms control this summary.

## What revenue counts?

Count the business and its controlled group before operating expenses,
marketplace fees and commissions. Exclude collected sales taxes, customer
refunds and duplicate intercompany revenue. Count all business activities,
rather than assigning a share of revenue to the SDK.

An individual excludes unrelated employment wages and unrelated personal
investment income. Capital raised and loan proceeds are not revenue. A new
business uses actual revenue since formation, without annualizing it.

For a client-owned application, use the higher revenue of the service provider
and the client group. The client holds the license and its contractors work
under it. Selling your own app to a large customer does not make that customer
your licensing client.

## What can I ship?

You may build and sell closed-source end-user apps, internal tools and services.
Use the supplied binaries, interfaces and documentation. If an SDK deliberately
includes source, you may compile and modify that source for integration.
No disclosure of your own application source is required by the SDK license.

A paid order identifies the SDK and product family; the same product on Mac,
iPhone and iPad is one family. Fees are fixed in the order, with no royalties
or charges per employee, render, document, installation or end user. The normal
term is twelve months. A bundle must identify the SDKs it includes.

Contact [licensing@swiftpython.dev](mailto:licensing@swiftpython.dev) for an Indie,
Business or OEM order. Community use requires neither payment nor a separate
order. All tiers can evaluate locally without charge before production use.

## What happens when revenue grows?

Community users have 90 days after reaching USD 100,000 to obtain the applicable
paid license for ongoing use and new distribution. Previously shipped end-user
copies continue to run.

An Indie user reaching USD 1,000,000 has 90 days to move to Business for rights
beyond already acquired versions and the covered product family. Paid-version
rights remain intact. No retroactive charge applies to previously permitted use.

## What happens if I do not renew?

Paid users keep the right to use and distribute the versions acquired during
their paid term for the covered product family, including later maintenance
releases of their app built with those versions. Renewal buys new SDK versions
and any agreed support. Existing installations do not need online activation.

## Is implementation source included?

Only source deliberately supplied in the SDK is included. Access to unpublished
implementation source, private tooling or private repositories requires a
separate written agreement. The commercial license does not promise that access
or make previously public information confidential.

## Can I redistribute an SDK or hosted developer service?

A separate OEM agreement is required to redistribute the SDK itself or expose
it as a service for other developers to incorporate into their products.
Ordinary end-user apps and services are covered by the application license,
including apps that compete with the licensor's own end-user products.

## Do earlier licenses and third-party terms change?

No. Material previously supplied under MIT, AGPL or another agreement retains
its applicable grants. This license applies to new rights offered with it.
Published releases keep the terms supplied with those releases.

Third-party code and fonts retain their licenses, notices and source obligations.
The commercial license does not override them or require disclosure of unrelated
private code.

## Examples

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Solo developer with USD 80,000 business revenue and an unrelated salary | Community |
| Solo developer with exactly USD 100,000 business revenue | Indie |
| Business with USD 999,999 business revenue | Indie |
| Business with exactly USD 1,000,000 business revenue | Business |
| Twenty-person business with USD 60,000 revenue | Community |
| Subsidiary with USD 20,000 revenue in a USD 5 million group | Business |
| Contractor building a client-owned app for a USD 5 million client | Business; client holds the license |
| Community developer selling their own app to a large company | Community; ordinary app sale |
| Developer redistributing the engine as an SDK with no revenue yet | OEM agreement |
